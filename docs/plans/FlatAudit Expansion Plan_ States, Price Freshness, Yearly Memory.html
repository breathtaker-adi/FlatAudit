Your questions, answered

Detect rent or sale

Combine independent signals and show “can’t tell” when they disagree, with a one-tap switch the user can set. Details in Phase 0.

Sudden price hikes

Compare a recent window to a baseline, using repeat listings and like-for-like groups, and show “market moved” with sample sizes. Details in Phase 1.

Remember every year

Keep raw listings for a short time, but keep monthly area statistics for years, plus dated data files. Details in Phase 1.

APIs for price history

No free official source I know of gives per-listing price history for Indian properties. Area averages come from your own backend; official indices add context. Details below.

Design for it: add a PriceSource interface (getAreaStats(cell, type, bhk), getIndex(city, type)). Your backend implements it today; an official index or a licensed provider can plug in later without changing the extension. Always label each number with its source, level and date.

Plan (about 12–14 weeks, one state at a time)

Phase 0: Truth audit and rent/sale detection

about 1–2 weeks
  • Check every pilot project, guidance value and cost rule against its official source; add source, verified_on, effective_from; drop what you can’t verify
  • Rent/sale classifier: URL words, breadcrumb and title, price units (per month, Lakh, Cr), presence of deposit or RERA/possession, portal-specific markers
  • Score signals; require at least two independent agreeing signals, otherwise mode = unknown
  • Unknown shows a mode switch; remember the user’s choice per listing key
  • Edge cases: PG and shared rooms, plots, commercial, lease, “rent or sale” ads; mark unsupported ones
  • Test on 100+ real pages per portal, in several cities; target 98%+ correct mode, with unknown allowed but wrong not
  • Rewrite the circle-rate message in neutral wording
Done when: wrong-mode results are rare, unknown is handled gracefully, and all shipped data has a source and date.

Phase 1: Price freshness and yearly memory

about 2–3 weeks
  • Keep raw observations for about 90 days only, then drop them
  • Table cell_month: cell, month, type, BHK, property type, status, n, median, p25, p75, MAD, price per sq ft median; keep for years, publish only when contributors ≥ k
  • Archive monthly packs (packs/YYYY-MM/); the extension caches the last 13 months for a cell for year-over-year views
  • Recency-weighted benchmark: recent listings count more; shorter window for rent than for sale; tune on real data
  • Spike check: compare the last 30 days with the prior 90 days per cell; require enough samples in both; show “moved +X% (n=A/B)”
  • Use repeat-listing price changes as the main evidence, plus like-for-like groups, so new premium listings don’t fake a hike
  • When the market is moving fast, widen the “typical range” and lower confidence instead of flagging more listings
  • Rent seasonality: compare against the same months last year once available
  • Cap each contributor’s influence and dedupe per listing per day to resist skew and fake data
  • Local watchlist: keep full detail for recent months, weekly points for older ones; CSV backup
  • Data files (stamp duty, guidance values) carry effective_from and valid_until; show “may be outdated” after a year and set a yearly review reminder
Done when: an area page shows current median, 30-day movement, 12-month trend, sample sizes and last update.

Metrics

  • Mode detection: % correct, % unknown, % wrong (target near zero wrong).
  • Field extraction per state: % of key fields matching the official page.
  • Freshness: median age of the data behind each verdict; share of verdicts with a visible sample size.
  • Spike check: back-test on any period where you know prices moved; count false alarms.

Risks

Stale or wrong official data: portals and rates change. Dates, sources and “may be outdated” warnings are mandatory.
Cold start in smaller cities: most verdicts will say “not enough data”. That is the correct answer.
Too many states: one person can’t maintain many portal readers. Go sequential and stop when upkeep exceeds your time.
Index misuse: a city index is not a flat’s value. Keep them visibly separate.
